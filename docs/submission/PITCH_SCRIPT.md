## Script

### 0:00–0:25 — The problem

> Crypto businesses should not have to publish who they pay. Yet a transparent treasury payment can reveal a vendor relationship, amount, cadence, and clues about the company’s operations. Meanwhile, bills, approvals, wallet transactions, and accounting evidence live in different tools. Privacy and financial control are treated as separate problems.

### 0:25–0:50 — Customer and solution

> Obliq is private accounts payable for crypto-native organizations paying vendors and contractors from a self-controlled treasury. Each payable moves through Capture, Control, Settle, Reconcile, and Prove. Finance manages the obligation, the vendor confirms a shielded destination, treasury retains signing authority, and an independent read-only observer closes the record.

### 0:50–1:20 — Why Zcash and why Obliq

> Zcash is fundamental here, not a checkout option. Shielded settlement can keep the counterparty, amount, and payment cadence off the public chain. A wallet can send Zcash, but it does not know whether an invoice is duplicated or a destination changed after approval. Obliq binds those controls to the exact payable while separating business approval, signing, and observation.

> Our Private Payables Link lets the intended vendor contact confirm one Orchard-capable destination for one obligation. It is not payment authority: a different finance reviewer must verify that immutable destination version.

### 1:20–1:43 — Differentiation and defensibility

> The link is not the moat. The defensible system connects obligation provenance, versioned policy, separation of duties, an immutable settlement intent, external signing, UFVK-only reconciliation, and selective evidence. No single component needs unrestricted treasury authority, and a receipt does not expose the wider payment history.
